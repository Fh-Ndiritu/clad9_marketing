# Platform playbooks

Both Pages were created on 2026-09-08 and start from zero followers. Everything below assumes a cold audience.

## Universal rules

- **Stage, never submit.** Write the copy, attach the image, leave the composer open. The user presses Post. This applies to posts and comments alike.
- **Never publish, follow, like, connect, invite or message** on the user's behalf.
- **Read the last 10 posts on the Page before writing** — this is the de-duplication mechanism. No repeated angle, no repeated opening construction.
- **One idea per post.** The voice is corrective and specific; two ideas dilute both.
- **No hashtag walls.** Facebook: none. LinkedIn: at most three, at the end.
- **No emoji strings.** At most one, usually zero.
- Every post must be traceable to a real page on clad9.com. If you cannot name the source URL, the claim is not grounded — rewrite it.

## Facebook Page

**URL:** facebook.com/clad9 (fallback `facebook.com/profile.php?id=61594427152424`)

**The identity trap — check this every single time.** The Page runs under the personal profile "Dennis Mutahi", which also administers the Hadaa.app Page. **Every new tab defaults to the Hadaa.app Page identity.** Before typing anything:
1. Click the avatar, top right.
2. Switch to the Clad9 Page.
3. Confirm the composer avatar shows the **C9 monogram** — cream background, maroon and gold C9.
Posting Clad9 copy as Hadaa is the single worst failure mode here. Verify visually, not by assumption.

**Shape that works on a cold Facebook Page:**
- Open with the correction or the observation. No preamble, no "Did you know".
- 60–120 words. Facebook truncates around 250 characters — the hook must land before the fold.
- One image, 3:4 portrait, attached natively.
- Soft close. Facebook suppresses outbound links in reach, so **put the link in the first comment**, not the post body. The post body can say "link below" or nothing at all.
- Line breaks between short paragraphs. Dense blocks don't get read.

**Staging steps:** open the Page → switch identity → click the composer ("What's on your mind?") → type the copy → attach the image via Photo/video → **stop**. Leave it open, tell the user it's ready.

## LinkedIn Page

**URL:** linkedin.com/company/clad9

**Post as the Page, not as Francis.** In the composer, the identity selector at the top must read Clad9. LinkedIn defaults to the personal profile.

**Shape that works:**
- 120–200 words. LinkedIn rewards a bit more length than Facebook.
- The first two lines are everything — LinkedIn truncates at "…see more" around 200 characters. Put the correction in line one.
- Short paragraphs, one or two sentences each, blank line between. This is the native LinkedIn rhythm and it suits the voice.
- One image, 1:1.
- LinkedIn also suppresses outbound links. Same rule: **link in the first comment**.
- At most three hashtags at the end. Sensible set: `#AIStyling` `#CapsuleWardrobe` `#SustainableFashion` — vary them.
- No "Thoughts?" / "Agree?" engagement bait. It reads as desperate and the voice is above it.

**Staging steps:** open the Page → Admin view → start a post → confirm the identity selector says Clad9 → type → attach image → **stop**.

## Comments

Commenting is how a zero-follower Page gets seen at all. It matters more than posting in the first month.

**Where to look:**
- LinkedIn: posts about capsule wardrobes, personal styling, sustainable fashion, decision fatigue, personal colour analysis, and AI-in-fashion. Also founders posting about their own wardrobe apps — the category is friendly.
- Facebook: fashion, minimalism, capsule wardrobe, decluttering and sustainable-style Groups and Pages.

**What makes a comment worth posting:**
- It adds something the post didn't say. A mechanism, a distinction, a specific.
- It stands alone as useful even if nobody clicks through.
- It never mentions Clad9 unless someone directly asks what tool to use.
- It is 2–4 sentences. Long comments read as ads.
- It never opens with "Great post!" or any variant.

**Hard gates — reject the post entirely if:**
- It is a personal milestone, a bereavement, a job loss, or anything where a brand comment would be crass.
- It is political, or a live controversy.
- It is someone's before/after photo of their own body.
- The account is a direct competitor whose post is about their own launch.
- The post is more than 3 days old — the comment will not be seen.

**Volume:** 4–5 comments per run. Open each in its own tab, staged unsubmitted, and hand them over together.
